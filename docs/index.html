Why 5%?

Small enough to afford. Big enough to change a winter.

5% is roughly one working day a month. For most of us it means one fewer takeaway, a cheaper phone plan or a skipped impulse buy. For a displaced family it can mean a blanket, a winter coat for a child, a food parcel or a tent that keeps the rain out.

One person's 5% helps. When thousands of us give it together, entire camps get through the winter. Faiths have always asked the same of us: zakat is 2.5% and the tithe is 10%. 5% sits between them, a fair share in an emergency.

    Questions

    Common questions

    Does this website collect donations?

    No. We send you straight to each charity's official website, so your whole donation goes to them, along with any tax relief or Gift Aid you're entitled to.

    I can't afford 5%. Should I still give?

    Yes. 5% is a goal, not a rule. 1% or 2% given regularly still buys blankets, food and medicine. Sharing this page with people who can give more helps too.

    Which charity should I choose?

    Local organisations often know their communities best and reach places others can't. Large international agencies bring scale and logistics. Splitting your 5% between one of each works well. Unrestricted donations, given without a specific project attached, let charities respond fastest.

    Is monthly giving better than a one-off gift?

    Monthly gifts let charities plan ahead and buy winter supplies in bulk before prices rise. If you can, set up a monthly donation from December to February.

    Can I give my zakat or sadaqah through these charities?

    Several listed organisations, including Islamic Relief, Human Appeal and Muslim Hands, run zakat-compliant funds. Check each charity's zakat policy on their website.

    Where do the photos come from?

    Every photo on this page is a real photograph from the place it describes, taken by humanitarian photographers and journalists and published under an open licence. Full credits are at the bottom of the page.
